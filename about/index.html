I grew up making things long before I knew what product design was.

My story

I spent most of my childhood with a paintbrush, a sewing needle, or a camera in my hand. Making things were never just hobbies. They were how I paid attention to the world.

I studied Computer Science because I wanted to understand how things worked underneath. Then I spent years in software engineering and technology consulting, building systems, reading error logs, and learning what it takes to ship something real.

Somewhere along the way, I realized the problems I cared about most were not technical. They were human. The moment someone hesitates before clicking. The interface that makes a complicated process feel possible. That is where product design found me.

Today, I design for confidence and clarity, especially in moments that feel unfamiliar. I want people to move forward feeling capable, not overwhelmed.

How I got here

Drawing was how I learned to translate what I noticed into something real.

Before interfaces, I learned that every detail changes how something feels.

Photography taught me to slow down and notice what other people overlook.

Every frame taught me that what you leave out matters as much as what you keep.

Most of my best design ideas started as notes in the margins.

Reading taught me to sit inside someone else's perspective.

Computer Science taught me how systems behave before I learned why people do.

Working with horses taught me patience, trust, and calm under uncertainty.

Product design became the place where making, systems, and understanding people finally met.

Design philosophy

The best products don't make people think harder. They help people feel more capable.
